## Who We Are Reaching Out To

There are two types of leads in this system:

### Type 1 — Early Stage (pre-seed, seed, Series A)
Founders building a startup who need help structuring their first or second raise.
They are looking for: capital structure advice, investor introductions, pitch narrative sharpening.
They may not have institutional investors yet.

### Type 2 — Growth Stage (Series B, growth, pre-IPO)
These come from the lead research pipeline (see PIPELINE_INSTRUCTIONS.md).
They are Indian companies with Rs 50 Crore+ revenue.
They are PE-backed or VC-backed already.
They are NOT looking for their first cheque.
They need: next-round advisory, secondary transactions, institutional co-investor introductions,
structured debt, IPO preparation, cap table management.

**Do not talk to a growth-stage founder the way you talk to a pre-seed founder.**
A company that has already raised a Series C from General Catalyst does not need to be told
"we help founders structure their raise." They have done that. They need something more specific.

## What the Email Is Trying to Do

The outreach email has one job: get a reply or a calendar booking.

It is NOT a pitch deck. It is NOT a newsletter. It is NOT a marketing email.
It should look and feel like a real person typed it on a laptop and hit send.

The tone is: informed, direct, warm but not overfamiliar.
The length is: short. Three short paragraphs maximum.
The design is: plain text. No logo. No colours. No buttons. No images.

---

## Email Structure

```
Hi [FirstName],

[One line: how we found them / why we are reaching out — specific to the company]

[One short paragraph: what we know about their specific situation,
 referencing real data from the notes field if available.
 For growth stage: speak to the next capital event, not the basics of fundraising.
 For early stage: speak to getting the structure right from the start.]

[One line: what Akro does and why it is relevant to THEM specifically]

If any of this is relevant, happy to spend 15 minutes on a call.
No prep needed on your end. Here is a link to find a time that works: [Calendly URL]

Rohit Jain
Akro Ventures
info@akroventures.com | akroventures.com
```

---

## Rules for the Email Content

1. NO em dashes (—) anywhere. Not in subject, not in body, not in signature.
2. NO fake "Re:" in subject line. It is a spam trigger and founders see through it.
3. Subject line format: `FirstName, a thought on CompanyName`
4. From name: `Rohit from Akro Ventures`
5. The notes field contains real intelligence about the company. USE it.
   Do not write a generic paragraph when you have specific data.
6. Do not use the word "founders" when addressing a growth-stage company.
   They are executives, not startup founders pitching at demo day.
7. Do not say "we help founders at every stage" — sounds like you help everyone = you specialise in nothing.
8. Do not repeat the same idea in two consecutive paragraphs.
9. No exclamation marks. No "hope this finds you well." No "I wanted to reach out."
10. The Calendly link is plain text inline, not a button.

## What "notes" Contains (for pipeline leads)

When a lead comes from the pipeline research, the notes field contains analyst-written intelligence like:

"PE-backed FM platform scaling to 125+ cities; advisory opportunity for next capital raise or strategic partnerships"
"Rs 290Cr Series B; 4x revenue growth; 35000+ women partner network across 21000 villages"
"7x customer growth; $500M valuation; $63M Series C from General Catalyst"

The first clause (before the semicolon) is the specific signal about the company.
Use it as a reference point in the email. Do not paste it verbatim — rephrase it naturally.

## The Approval Flow

Before any email reaches a founder, Rohit or Akshita reviews it.
The internal team receives an approval email with:
- Full deal details
- AI-generated internal summary (strengths, risks, recommended service)
- The draft founder email exactly as it will be sent
- Three buttons: Approve and Send / Edit and Send / Reject

Whatever the team approves is exactly what gets sent. The system never regenerates content after approval.

## Why These Emails Are Not Getting Replies (honest assessment)

Generic content is the main reason. If the email could have been sent to any of 500 companies,
it will be ignored by all 500. The only emails that get replies are the ones where the founder
reads the first two sentences and thinks "this person actually looked at us."

The fix is specificity. Use what you know about the company.
Reference something real. Speak to where they actually are, not where a generic founder is.
